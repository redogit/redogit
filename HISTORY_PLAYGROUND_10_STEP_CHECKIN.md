# History Playground — 10-Step Check-In

**Purpose:** explore years of projects, theories, formal results, experiments, tools, games, apps, education, philosophy, ethics, coding, failures, and recovered memories **without centralizing them into one authority**.

This is a learning quest, not a completeness certificate.

> **10/10 means you opened ten useful doors. It does not mean the whole historical corpus has been exhausted.**

An earlier Library review inventoried **3,440 file identities** and about **4.72 GB** of stored material, but deeply reviewed only a bounded subset. Historical recovery must therefore remain repeatable, source-aware, and honest about gaps.

## How every step works

Each stop uses the same five moves:

1. **FIND** — locate one real artifact, app, game, proof, experiment, or history record.
2. **TRY** — play with it, run it, inspect it, or perform the tiny challenge.
3. **LEARN** — identify one supported result and one boundary/failure.
4. **TEACH** — explain the distinction to another willing learner in your own words.
5. **LEAVE A TRAIL** — preserve a source link, note, question, or next experiment without overwriting the predecessor.

Teaching is optional and learner-directed. The learner's goal comes first. A teach-back is not an authority transfer.

### Tiny challenge
Take two short versions of a paragraph or idea. Find:

- what changed;
- what did not change;
- what cannot be inferred from a textual difference alone.

### Learn
`newer != replacement`

`reconstruction != source`

A handoff can preserve evidence and chronology without inheriting authority.

### Check-in

# Step 3 — Language, Memory, and Knowledge

### Theme
Orbit, Knowledge Decay, Tiny Babel, TBCL, CSOL, Language Structure Workbench, Musilanguage, source/witness/translation carriers.

### Try
- [Orbit Shelf](https://redogit.github.io/conscience64/play/orbit/)
- [Word Weave](https://redogit.github.io/conscience64/play/weave/)
- [Computational Chorus](https://redogit.github.io/conscience64/play/computational-chorus/)

### Tiny challenge
Store one idea in two carriers—for example ordinary prose and a compact mnemonic. Ask:

- what meaning survived?
- what effort changed?
- what provenance was lost or retained?

### Learn
Tiny Babel/TBCL produced bounded finite verification results; those results do not establish a universal language. Orbit experiments showed that active knowledge can be much smaller than preserved experience while richer evidence remains recoverable.

The older CSOL line tracks clarity, consistency, economy, composability, reliability, and human reconstruction burden.

### Teach it forward
Explain one idea twice: once for precision, once for ease of recall. Let the learner tell you which carrier serves their goal better.

### Check-in
- [ ] I can explain `carrier != subject`.
- [ ] I preserved enough source material to correct the compact version later.

# Step 5 — Build, Compile, Break, Repair

### Theme
Coding, algorithmic builder/compiler work, optimizer-of-optimizers, LLVM/MLIR/WASM, ECS, REDOGIT successors, mutation testing, MauiBrickBreak, FirstNeuralNetwork.

### Try
- [MauiBrickBreak](https://github.com/redogit/MauiBrickBreak)
- [FirstNeuralNetwork](https://github.com/redogit/FirstNeuralNetwork)
- Browse [Other Projects](https://github.com/redogit/Other-Projects-) for current compiler/search experiments.

### Tiny challenge
For any small program or algorithm, write:

`input → operation → observable output → verifier`

Then deliberately mutate one rule and make sure the test actually exercises the mutation.

### Learn
One retained mutation-method correction changed an apparent **5/7** result into a valid **7/7** only after proving the mutations were active. The rule became:

`mutation not proven active -> mutation result inadmissible`

### Teach it forward
Give someone a tiny bug and a test that *fails to notice it*. Let them improve the test before fixing the bug.

### Check-in
- [ ] My test can fail for the intended reason.
- [ ] I separated implementation success from broader theory claims.

# Step 6 — Minds, Models, Agents, and Societies

### Theme
Operational Closure Theory of Computer Mind, Computer Mind Lab, SHADOW, PulseNet, SPDM, streaming memory, agent/skill/society/swarm work.

### Try
- Read the public Conscience64 skills/society material.
- Compare a model mechanism against a simpler baseline.
- Inspect a preserved failure before reading its successor.

### Tiny challenge
Pick a claimed mechanism and identify:

- what observation would support it;
- what lesion/ablation would challenge it;
- what simpler mechanism could imitate the result.

### Learn
The Computer Mind program deliberately preserved mixed evidence: early local probes passed in a purpose-built toy; later architecture/metacontrol gates failed; a narrower selection-plus-consumption component later passed its locked test. None of that establishes consciousness, personhood, or general intelligence.

### Teach it forward
Ask the learner to explain why a system producing intelligent-looking behavior is not, by itself, a causal test of the proposed mechanism.

### Check-in
- [ ] I found both a success and a failure in the lineage.
- [ ] I can say what the experiment did **not** establish.

# Step 7 — Mathematics: Proof, Computation, and Open Problems

### Theme
R³ formal work, Hodge conjecture, P versus NP, SAT/Independent Set, SEARCH/CERTIFY, boundary quotients, decision fields, exact enumeration, SMT/Lean verification.

### Try
- Inspect [Other Projects](https://github.com/redogit/Other-Projects-) research branches.
- Inspect Conscience64's Hodge research record.
- Read an R³ theorem artifact and its verification boundary.

### Tiny challenge
Take one mathematical statement and label it exactly one of:

`PROVED` · `EXHAUSTIVELY VERIFIED FINITE` · `COMPUTATION` · `COUNTEREXAMPLE` · `HYPOTHESIS` · `OPEN`

Then state what would be needed to move it one rung higher.

### Learn
Examples from the recovered work:

- R³ has independently compiled Lean core kernels in part of the lineage, while other stronger theorem files retain separate replication gates.
- A sealed R³ union gate exhaustively checked an exact finite 11-case union but explicitly did **not** claim population-wide proof.
- Hodge work has explicit finite character calculations, constructions, descent arguments, and remaining geometric obligations; the general Hodge conjecture remains open.
- P-vs-NP experiments have produced useful exact reductions and negative results; neither `P = NP` nor `P != NP` is established.

### Teach it forward
Give the learner one result and one overclaim. Ask them to identify which word makes the overclaim unjustified.

### Check-in
- [ ] I can separate theorem validity, encoding validity, and verifier validity.
- [ ] I preserved the open-problem claim ceiling.

# Step 8 — Science and the Physical World

### Theme
Physics / GR–quantum seam, observation vs interpretation, physical engineering, water/pool work, sensors, controlled experiments, confounds.

### Try
- Inspect a physics distinction/counter-probe record.
- Inspect the pool/water design lineage.
- Pick a physical claim and write a measurement that could be wrong for at least two different reasons.

### Tiny challenge
For a phenomenon, write:

`raw observation → comparison → interpretation`

Then invent one confound that changes the interpretation without changing the raw observation.

### Learn
The work repeatedly separates physical/structural obstruction from knowledge/representation obstruction. Pool-cleaner work remains a design/engineering line unless a physical prototype is actually tested; geometry or model curvature does not become spacetime evidence by analogy.

### Teach it forward
Have the learner propose a control that distinguishes two competing explanations.

### Check-in
- [ ] I kept measurement separate from interpretation.
- [ ] I named at least one confound or alternate explanation.

# Step 9 — Accessibility, Education, and Teaching

### Theme
CPACC, UDL, Section 508, JAWS, accessible documents, Excel/report tooling, Operator Moonshot Education E1/E2/E3, learner-first teaching.

### Try
- Review a CPACC/UDL topic using an accessible format.
- Test a page or tool by keyboard/screen-reader-oriented structure rather than sight alone.
- Teach a willing learner one bounded concept and let their response change your explanation.

### Tiny challenge
Use the UDL anchors:

- Engagement — **why**;
- Representation — **what**;
- Action & Expression — **how**.

Explain one of the earlier technical ideas through all three.

### Learn
The recovered education experiments provide a useful scientific lesson: E1/E2 synthetic diagnostic work passed bounded gates, while E3 failed robustness. Successful diagnosis did **not** imply robust causal instruction.

The learner-first Orbit rule is:

`explicit learning intent → learner goal → bounded retrieval → teach → learner response → adapt → goal completion → release`

The goal is transferable knowledge, not dependency.

### Teach it forward
Let the learner teach the same idea back in a different representation. Their version does not need to copy yours.

### Check-in
- [ ] The learner's goal, access needs, and agency remained primary.
- [ ] I did not confuse quiz performance with a complete model of learning.

Make something that is allowed to be **just fun**: a pattern, song, story fragment, game route, weird operator, puzzle, or tiny world.

Play can be a generator of questions without becoming evidence. Game canon, generated images, stories, mnemonics, and scientific records retain separate authority.

### Teach it forward
Give someone the artifact **without the explanation first**. Let them play. Only teach when they ask or when teaching serves their chosen goal.

- [ ] I can point to its lineage without claiming it proves more than it does.

## The finish line

There is no global graduation.

After Step 10 you can:

- loop back to any step;
- pick a different project;
- teach one idea;
- build a new app;
- reopen a failure;
- recover an old conversation or file;
- add a new typed bridge;
- or stop and do something else.

**The history is a playground with evidence boundaries, not a maze with one correct exit.**
